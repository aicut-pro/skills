# 3D with a GLSL shader

EXPLAINER STUDIO, 3D WITH A GLSL SHADER. Clay, toy, vinyl, low-poly, voxel, brick, knit and metal looks are real 3D: raymarched signed distance fields in the template's `ShaderLayer`, rendered by Chrome's software GL (`swangle`) in `aicut sandbox exec`, with no generated images. Keep the GLSL in files next to `Video.tsx` and import them (a `.glsl` import is a string), after the template's library:
import SDF from "./lib/sdf.glsl"; import HERO from "./hero.glsl";
const params = { cam: interpolate(frame, [0, 120], [0, 1], { extrapolateRight: "clamp" }), wave: 0 };
<ShaderLayer glsl={SDF + HERO} params={params} />

HOW THE SHADER RUNS
- The file defines `vec2 map(vec3 p)` (x = the distance, y = a material id; `sdf.glsl` declares it and its `march`, `calcNormal`, `softShadow` and `calcAO` call it) and `vec4 pixel(vec2 uv)`; the wrapper owns `main()`. Write GLSL ES 1.00: constant loop bounds; no `texture()`, `round()`, `trunc()`, `tanh()`, `inverse()` or `transpose()`; no `%` or bit operators; globals initialised only with constants.
- `uv` runs 0..1 across the frame with y pointing down. `u_resolution` is the frame size in pixels. `u_time` is the seconds since the layer's own scene started, not the video's global time: subtract nothing.
- Every key of `params` becomes a float uniform `u_<key>` (`cam` is `u_cam`); not `time` or `resolution`. Compute the values from the frame with `interpolate` or `spring`, which drives camera moves, poses and reveals; a param the GLSL already eases moves linearly.
- The output is premultiplied alpha. Captions, numerals and HUD stay HTML and SVG placed after the `ShaderLayer` in the same scene.
- The library (`src/lib/sdf.glsl`): `sdSphere`, `sdCapsule`, `sdRoundBox`, `smin`, `opU`, `lin` and `toSrgb`, `cameraRay`, `march` (96 steps), `calcNormal`, `softShadow` (32 steps), `calcAO` (5 taps) and `shade` (key light, sky fill, gloss-driven highlight and rim). Write any other primitive or look (an ellipsoid, a cylinder, a torus, facets, a knit, a studio environment, a grade, grain) in your own file.
- Cost: measure one still of the scene before the render (the build reference's estimate). A simple raymarched scene takes about 0.5 to 1.5 s per 1080x1920 frame on a busy 4-core machine; a detailed character takes several times longer. Keep those step counts and model with a few dozen primitives, not hundreds.

MATCH THE REFERENCE, NOT A GENERIC RENDER
- CHARACTER. Build a new hero in the reference's construction, not a copy of its character: the same head-shape family (sphere, bean, box), proportions and level of detail. Give it a nose, eyes with catchlights, brows, a mouth, a hair mass, costume details (collar, tie, piping, pockets, hems) and real hands, as the reference does. A missing face detail or a costume flattened to one colour is the most visible miss.
- MATERIAL AND LIGHT. Write palette hex as `lin(vec3(r, g, b) / 255.0)`. Matte clay is low gloss with strong AO, and vinyl is high gloss plus rim. Faceted low-poly: flat normals from a coarse grid of the position. Voxel or brick: rounded boxes on a `floor()` grid. Metal: the albedo times a simple studio environment of the reflected ray. Knit: a small periodic bump on the normal along two axes.
- LENS AND DEPTH OF FIELD. For a blurred background, split the scene into two shader layers: a backdrop (under a CSS `filter: blur(10px to 20px)`, which may be drawn at 540x960 in a frame scaled 2x) and the hero (sharp, contact shadow only).
- GRADE. Haze, gamma and lifted blacks on lit surfaces in your own `grade()`; add grain only when the reference has grain.
